# Animation narration scripts for recording

Record these in the same voice as the lesson narrations. One audio file per animation, with a pause of about 1.5 seconds between the parts. Send the files (WAV is fine) and they will be compressed and wired in. The wording below replaces the wording in the current animation recordings.

## Introduction animation (Lesson 1)

Part 1
Space science is the study of space: the Sun, the Moon, the planets and the stars. It also means the machines we send up there, like satellites.

Part 2
You use space science every day, even if you do not notice. Weather forecasts, phone maps and navigation, and some TV and phone signals all depend on satellites.

Part 3
Ghana is part of this too. In 2012, Ghana opened its own space science institute. In 2017, Ghana's first satellite, GhanaSat-1, was built by Ghanaian engineers. A rocket carried it to the International Space Station, and it was released into orbit from there.

Part 4
Satellites also help us look after our land: our coast, our farms and our growing towns. So let us use one to look at a place you may know: Pokuase.

## Satellite animation (Lesson 7)

Part 1
Let us see how a satellite records a picture of Pokuase. High above Ghana, a satellite travels around the Earth. It is about 786 kilometres up.

Part 2
The Sun shines on the ground, and some of that light bounces back up. The satellite has an instrument that measures it.

Part 3
The picture is made of tiny squares called pixels. Each pixel covers an area about 10 metres by 10 metres on the ground, roughly the length of a bus on each side.

Part 4
Sometimes clouds block the view. So the satellite records many pictures over one year, and they are blended into one clear picture.

Part 5
Now look at two years side by side, 2017 and 2025. Find the places that changed.

What changed and why: "takes a picture" and "photos" became "records" and "pictures", because a satellite measures light and is not an ordinary camera. "Each square covers 10 metres" became "each pixel covers an area about 10 metres by 10 metres", because a square covers an area, not a length.
